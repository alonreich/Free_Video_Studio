## 1. Master Application Volume Control  {#AUD-MASTERVOL}
* **One Master, One Rack (VOLSHARED_01):** The vertical fluid "test tube" slider (`FluidVolumeSlider`), the % badge and the speaker button of the **Main App**, **Video Merger** and **Crop Tools** are all wired by ONE helper, `MasterVolumeUi.Bind`. The suite master is `MpvIpcClient.GlobalMasterVolume` (0–100) + `GlobalMuted`; every change (from any window, the keyboard, the wheel or Windows) fires `GlobalMasterVolumeChanged`, and every bound window repaints its slider/badge/icon (re-entrancy guarded) AND re-applies its own players. A window never re-sends its slider value as the master (that is how a stale Main slider used to snap the level back after a trip to the Crop Tools).
* **Shared Mute (VOLMUTE_01):** the speaker button toggles `GlobalMuted` in all three apps at once; the level is kept and restored exactly. Moving the level while muted unmutes. Unmuting a 0% master restores 50%. Muted shows `MUTE` on the badge.
* **Persistence:** `MasterVolumePersistence` saves the level (session state `MainVolume`) and the mute (settings `PreviewMuted`) 600 ms after ANY change, off the UI thread. Seeded at startup (`AvaloniaApp.StartSharedMasterVolume`).
* **Music Wizard fader persistence (WIZVOLDEBOUNCE_01):** the VIDEO/MUSIC faders follow the same rule. A drag changes only the in-memory value and the player gains on the UI thread; `WizardVideoVolume` / `WizardMusicVolume` are persisted by a 600 ms trailing-edge debounce through `StateTransferStore.UpdatePropertiesAsync` on the thread pool (non-blocking; no mutex wait or disk flush on the dispatcher). See `05` §4d.
* **Mouse Wheel (VOLWHEEL_01):** one notch = 5%, Shift = 1%.
* **Perceptual Curve (VOLCURVE_01):** master gain = (position/100)³ (mpv's own cubic curve: 50% ≈ -18 dB). The Music Wizard VIDEO/MUSIC faders stay LINEAR because they are the export's `volume=` gains. A player's mpv volume is:
  $$V_{\text{mpv}} = P_{\text{master}} \cdot \sqrt[3]{B_{\text{balance}}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{gain} = (P_{\text{master}}/100)^3 \cdot B$$
  (`MpvIpcClient.PlayerMpvVolume` / `ApplyPreviewGainAsync`). NAudio players (voice-over takes) use `MasterLinearGain`.
* **Windows Volume Mixer Sync (VOLSYNC_01):** while Core Audio is available, this process's default audio session CARRIES the master: app → session level (position/100)³ + session mute; Mixer → app position ∛level × 100 + shared mute (polled every 300 ms on one MTA thread, echo-suppressed). `MasterAppliedBySystem` is then true and players carry ONLY their balance, so the master is applied once. On any Core Audio failure the flag is false and players apply the master themselves. The session volume is linear amplitude (Microsoft: ISimpleAudioVolume is linear-tapered), hence the cube; the two sliders therefore show different numbers for the same loudness (app 79% ⇔ Mixer 50%).
* **Preview Only:** the master never reaches an FFmpeg export graph.

## 2. Audio Mastering & Peak Protection Pipeline  {#AUD-MASTERING}
* **No Loudness Standard (LOUDSTD_REMOVED_01):** clips, music, voice-over and preview keep their recorded level, scaled only by the user's faders. The -14 LUFS "industry standard" detection, its upload prompt, its setting (`LoudnessNormalizationPrompt`), the dead two-pass loudnorm machinery (`PerformLoudnormPassAsync`, `BuildLoudnormSecondPassFilter`, `VolumeNormalizeDb`, `MeasureMusicBedGainsAsync`, `QuietBoostReductionFactor`, `MusicBedLufs`, `TargetLufs`), the dead `AutoVoiceNormalization` setting and the preview balance offsets (`PREVIEW_03/04`) are deleted. `AudioLoudnessProbe` remains as a pure MEASUREMENT (loudnorm analysis into the null muxer, results cached per file/window) for exactly three consumers: harsh-peak detection, the tamer threshold, meme/clip matching.
* **Harsh-Peak Detection (kept):** on upload, `HasHarshPeaks` = crest (TP − I) > 15 LU AND TP > -1.5 dBTP → the "Sudden Loud Moment" prompt (Settings: Ask / Always / Never).
* **Peak Tamer (PEAKSAFE_01, user switch `AutoSpikeFlattening` × prompt answer):** on the GAMEPLAY bus only, before voice-over and music are mixed, threshold = measured integrated loudness + 9 LU (clamped -40…-6 dBFS):
  ```text
  acompressor=threshold={I+9}dB:ratio=6:attack=1:release=150:knee=2:makeup=1:detection=peak
  ```
  Main App: measured over the exported range (fallback: the upload reading). Merger: per clip, against that clip's own level. Measured: a +12 dB burst over -37 dB body was reduced 6.7 dB peak; the body is untouched. It never runs on the summed mix (a music bed mixed louder than the game would be squashed).
* **Safety Limiter (PEAKSAFE_01, ALWAYS ON, no switch):** on every export's final mix (Main and Merger):
  ```text
  aresample=192000,alimiter=limit=-2.3dB:attack=1:release=50:level_in=1:level_out=1:level=disabled:latency=1,aresample=48000
  ```
  ⚠️ **`latency=1` is load-bearing (AVSYNC_03).** `alimiter` looks ahead by its attack time and, uncompensated, emits everything that much late: the whole mix trailed the video by exactly 1.0 ms (bundled build: 1 kHz bursts at n s decoded at n + 0.0010 s; with `latency=1` at n + 0.0000 s, identical sample count).
  4× oversampled so it catches inter-sample peaks: measured -2.1 dBTP on hot program (ceiling `SafetyCeilingDbtp` = -2.0 dBTP).
  ⚠️ **`level=disabled` is load-bearing.** `alimiter`'s auto-level defaults ON and rescales by 1/limit: the old `alimiter=limit=-1.5dB` measured +1.5 dB louder on everything with peaks back at 0.0 dBFS (+1.0 dBTP), and was applied twice to voice-over (+3 dB) and to memes (+2 dB). Those per-take and per-meme limiters are removed.
* **Meme Level (MEMELEVEL_02):** gain = measured gameplay − measured meme (clamped -24…+12 dB); either unmeasured → as recorded. A CORNER OVERLAY meme with "Play meme sound" on gets the same gain and SPLICE_03 fades, delayed to its window and summed over the game audio (`amix normalize=0, duration=first`, 03 FFM-MEMECORNER); with sound off it contributes nothing (MEMEMODE_01). Same rule in the Main App (`ProcessWorker`) and the Merger (`MergeClipGraph`, against the host clip after clip matching).
* **De-click Splices (SPLICE_03):** 8 ms fade pairs (≤ 2% of the piece) on every audio piece that butt-joins an unrelated neighbour: both sides of every meme splice (both apps) and every Merger clip join.
* **Merger Clip Matching (CLIPLEVEL_01, Settings › Editing › Video Merger, default OFF):** each clip with sound is gained toward the queue's MEDIAN measured loudness, ±12 dB. Merger audio speed uses the shared `AudioTempoFilterBuilder` (TEMPO_01, `03_FFMPEG_EXPORT_PIPELINE.md` FFM-TEMPO): no filter at 1.0× (AVSYNC_01), Rubber Band below 1.0× when the bundled FFmpeg verifies it, atempo otherwise.

## 2b. The Preview Hears The Export (PREVIEWMIX_01 / VOPREVIEW_01)  {#AUD-PREVIEWMIX}
* **Rendered Mix:** whenever the Main App edit has music, voice-over, a meme, the tamer switched on (whether or not the upload reading exists — the export measures the exported range itself) or any rate other than 1.0x (TEMPO_01: the export's atempo / Rubber Band, which the live gameplay player's mpv scaletempo cannot reproduce; a freeze is silence, not a tempo) — `PreviewMixPolicy.NeedsRenderedMix` — the export's audio is rendered in the background: the SAME payload (`ComposeExportPayloadAsync`) and worker configuration (`MainMediaController.ApplyPayload`) as PROCESS, `ProcessWorker.AudioPreviewOutputPath` set, `AudioGraphPruner.Prune` keeps only the chains the final audio label depends on (the meme `concat v=1:a=1` becomes `v=0:a=1`, orphan pads get `anullsink`), output 48 kHz PCM WAV. Measured: rendered mix vs exported file's audio = -60 dB RMS difference (AAC noise).
* **Playback:** while the mix matches the edit (`PreviewMixSignature`), the gameplay, music and voice-over players are silenced and one audio-only player follows the video in output time: `AudioPreviewMap.MixSecFor(t) = intro + fade-in pad + t + Σ memes cut before`, seek-only drift correction (MUSICSYNC_02 tolerances). During a meme cutaway the tick returns early and the mix simply plays the meme section. Only FULL SCREEN memes are counted in `MixSecFor` (a corner overlay adds no time); a corner meme's sound is part of the rendered mix (MEMEMODE_01). Any edit drops the mix at once (live players take over); a new one renders 0.8 s after the edits stop.
* **Stale-Result Protection (PREVIEWMIX_02):** `RenderedMixGate` is the one owner of "which mix is valid". (1) The signature (every export-affecting audio input, including the per-track music lengths) is re-observed FIRST on every playback tick (`RefreshPreviewMixState`, before the music/voice followers and the mix player), not only on the 250 ms scheduler, so an edit silences the old mix before any further preview audio is driven. (2) Every render is a numbered ticket; it completes only if it is the newest AND the signature it rendered still equals the signature re-computed at completion (`TryComplete`); a payload composed while the edit moved is abandoned. (3) Renders alternate between two WAVs; starting one into the file the valid mix lives in invalidates that mix first (undoing back to its settings used to re-activate a half-overwritten file). (4) A failed render latches its signature (`FailedSignature`) — it is retried only after the next edit (it used to retry every 250 ms) — and the preview shows the fidelity marker (04 UI-PREVIEWFIDELITY) instead of claiming the export's sound. Pinned by `RenderedMixGateTests`.
* **Live Fallback (VOPREVIEW_01 / VOPREVIEW_02):** the voice-protection dip is a pure time pulse, so the live players reproduce it exactly (0.3 s ramps, 85%) with the export's own expression, `AudioFilterChain.VoiceProtectionPulseAt` = clip(Σ per-take ramps, 0, 1) — the SUM, as the export writes it (the live preview used the MAXIMUM, which dipped less where two takes' ramps overlap). The Voice Over studio applies the same dip to its gameplay player across its takes (`VoiceOverWindow.UpdatePreviewVoiceProtection`); its carve and the music protection need the rendered mix and are heard in the Main App preview: VOICE first, GAMEPLAY second, MUSIC last (VOPRIO_01). The gameplay player carries the tamer as an mpv `af=lavfi=[…]`. Ducking and carving need the game as a live sidechain and are heard only in the rendered mix — the Music Wizard's players carry no filter.

* **Cover selection is visual only (THUMBAUDIO_01):** setting, moving or removing the Main App's cover marker does not invalidate the rendered audio signature. The intro remains 0.1 s of silence for both automatic and manually selected cover frames. Audio-affecting edits still invalidate the mix normally.

## 2a. Cached Player State Must Not Lag The Command  {#AUD-IPCSTATE}
`MpvIpcClient` caches player state (`IsPaused`, `IsEof`, `CurrentTime`, `Duration`) from **asynchronous** libmpv property observers. Every consumer polls that cache from a UI tick. The rule that makes the cache safe:

> **A field the caller can invalidate by issuing a command is written LOCALLY at the moment that command is sent, not when the observer eventually agrees.**

`IsPaused` has always obeyed this. `IsEof` did not, and the consequence was a hard lock (MPVEOF_01):

* `MainWindow`'s tick ends with an end-of-file guard that pauses the player.
* PLAY issues `pause=no`; the 100 ms tick fires before mpv's `eof-reached=no` event lands; the tick reads a stale `true` and pauses again.
* One frame, then paused, indefinitely — reachable from **any** route that leaves the playhead at the end: playing to it, seeking to it, or dragging a marker there.

`IsEof` is therefore cleared locally on `pause=no` and on every `seek` command. mpv still owns the truth and the observer restores `true` the moment the file really does end. Anchoring rule for the surfaces that consume it: `01_TIMELINE_COORDINATE_MATH.md` §8 (TL-ENDSTOP).

---

**Seek completion (SEEKSETTLE_01):** `SendCommandAsync("seek", ...)` marks `IsSeeking` immediately, but command acceptance is NOT completion. `SeekCompleted` is emitted from libmpv's `MPV_EVENT_PLAYBACK_RESTART` (21), after `MPV_EVENT_SEEK` (20) and with native `seeking=no`; the position cache is refreshed first. End-file and rejected commands release the pending seek too. Repeated identical absolute seeks are suppressed while the first is decoding; relative user seeks retain their meaning. This lets the windows' existing latest-target coalescers wait for actual decode completion instead of restarting a slow seek on each tick. The Main App music/voice/mix followers and Merger music follower do not correct drift against a seeking video clock, or issue another correction while their audio player is seeking. Pausing or ending playback still stops followers immediately, even while a seek is pending. Existing settling delays and drift thresholds remain unchanged. Windows native regressions cover paused seeks, rapid scrubs, audio-only playback, Merger EDL reloads, rejection and stop cleanup (`MpvSeekCompletionTests`, opt in with `FVS_TEST_NATIVE_MPV=1`).

## 3. Sidechain Compression & Dynamic Ducking  {#AUD-SIDECHAIN}
* **Multiband (DUCKMB_01):** the music bed is split into three bands, the gameplay(+voice) is the sidechain:
  ```text
  [music]aformat=fltp:48000:stereo,acrossover=split='250 2900'[mus_low][mus_mid][mus_high]
  LOW  (<250 Hz)     untouched (the bed keeps its body)
  MID  (250-2900 Hz) ducking  sidechaincompress=threshold=0.1:ratio=4:attack=10:release=400:detection=peak
                     carving  sidechaincompress=threshold=0.1:ratio=2.5:attack=10:release=250:detection=peak
  HIGH (>2900 Hz)    ducking  (same as MID)
  [mus_low][mid][high]amix=inputs=3:weights='1 1 1':normalize=0
  ```
  The band split sums flat (measured ±0.001 dB, 60 Hz-12 kHz). Measured under a near-full-scale trigger: ducking -8.5 dB, carving -4 dB on the mid band, both -12 dB; nothing happens while the game is quiet. The old tuning (threshold 0.15, ratio 1.13, high band only) measured -1.2 dB — inaudible.
* **Trigger:** `highpass=f=200,lowpass=f=3500,agate=threshold=0.05:attack=5:release=100,aformat=…`, split once per compressor stage. Both sidechain inputs are format-pinned (otherwise ffmpeg rejects the graph: "No channel layout for input 1").
* **Switches & Strength Handles (DUCKSTRENGTH_01):** Settings › Sound & Music › "Music vs. game sound" has two checkboxes — **Music dips when the game gets loud** (ducking) and **Music makes room for game sounds** (EQ carving) (both ON by default; SETTIPS_01 plain labels) — and two vertical handles, **DIP** and **ROOM** (0-100, styled like the Music Wizard's MIX rack), each starting in the MIDDLE (50 = the tuned values above, label "Normal"; "+n"/"-n" either side; "Back to normal" resets both). Only the ratio moves, smoothly and per handle:
  $$\text{ratio}(h) = 1 + (\text{tuned} - 1)\cdot 2^{\frac{h-50}{50}\cdot 1.5}$$
  Ducking 2.06 · **4** · 9.49, carving 1.53 · **2.5** · 5.24 (each whole step < 4% change — no jumps). Sent to the graph as `ducking_strength` / `carving_strength`. A handle is disabled while its checkbox is off. Settings OFF wins over the Music Wizard's per-video checkbox (shown unticked and greyed with a pointer to Settings). Stored in `Defaults.DuckingEnabled/CarvingEnabled/DuckingStrength/CarvingStrength` (schema v13 seeds the switches once from the legacy `AudioProtection`; user values survive every later upgrade). View-model: `MixProtectionViewModel`.
* **Graph flags:** `ducking_enabled` and `carving_enabled` (Music Wizard checkbox AND the Settings switch). Both off → no split, no trigger, no crossover: the music reaches the mix untouched (DUCKOFF_01). Configs without the flag: a legacy ratio of 1.0 still means ducking off.
* **Carving Is Dynamic:** the static `equalizer=f=2000:width_type=h:width=1800:g=-4` (which dulled the music for the whole video, even over silence) is gone.

## 4. Voice Over Studio  {#AUD-VOICEOVER}
* **VOSAMPLE_01 — saved endpoint:** `VoiceCaptureBudget` freezes the prepared body output clock immediately before the recorder starts, after device/writer setup on the serialized worker. Every opening buffer belongs to that same anchor; delayed UI publication never reanchors it. The maximum sample-frame count is `floor(max(0, plan.Timeline.TotalOutputSeconds - actualOutputStart) * sampleRate)`. Byte limits multiply frames by the actual format's block alignment. Only the valid whole-frame prefix of a crossing buffer is written, counted and published to the meter; further drain buffers are ignored. Reaching the limit requests stop on the existing serialized device worker, never waits inside a capture callback. The UI maps this immutable anchor to source plus `OutputOffsetSec` (including freeze holds), and completion/recovery/Apply retain the accepted duration, including any positive sub-millisecond tail. A bounded open requested to finalize drains after its open verdict instead of deleting opening audio. Full-screen memes and intros are excluded from this body budget; `VoiceOverSplice` inserts their export gaps. This endpoint cap does not trim interrupted takes to the stall guard's safe-clock sample.
* **VOWRITE_01 — storage failure is capture failure:** a WAV write exception latches `CaptureError`, publishes one terminal fault and requests serialized stop. No further PCM writes are attempted. NAudio 3.1.0 acknowledges data length after a successful stream write; a partially written failed buffer is rolled back to the preceding acknowledged boundary before header finalization. Failure to repair/finalize remains an explicit file error for recovery. Subscriber exceptions are isolated from storage failures. A safely finalized positive prefix is retained and marked NEEDS REVIEW; it is never reported as a successful capture. A fault during open must retain positive opening audio and publish the anchor before fault handling. Stop/dispose are serialized and idempotent. Regression coverage uses the production recorder/adapter with synthetic `IWaveIn` and failing streams, without microphone hardware.
* **VOCLOCK_01 — prepared recording picture:** speed, cut and freeze timing comes from the same 60 fps graph compiler as export. Recording stays unavailable until rendering succeeds and mpv reports the expected file, positive duration, a usable clock and speed 1x. The proxy is local, cancellable and session-owned; it is removed only after preview shutdown succeeds. Speech plays at its natural recorded rate.
* **VOREC_INIT_01:** cached indicator geometry is parsed on first UI use; accessing recording state or recovery seams before Avalonia starts must not initialize the platform renderer.
* **VOSTALL_01 — capture-clock guard:** compare captured PCM seconds with advancing OUTPUT seconds, including rendered freeze holds. After a 300 ms startup grace, more than 150 ms drift, a backward jump over 30 ms, a seek or unexpected pause stops that recording segment. Preserve the complete raw WAV, mark it NEEDS REVIEW, pause the picture and resume from the last aligned output position. Never silently truncate the failed ending. Each resume creates a fresh anchored take.
* **VOTIMING_01 — subsequent edits:** speed, cut, freeze and trim changes that affect the spoken picture prompt **Cancel change** or **Keep change and review** after the gesture settles. Cancel restores timing; Keep preserves natural-speed audio and flags affected takes. Upstream changes that merely move an anchor, later changes and zoom-only edits do not flag a take. Save/open/undo/redo/close/export settle any pending decision; autosave waits. Export is blocked until flagged takes are reviewed in the studio. The user selects a marked take, listens, then chooses **KEEP THIS TIMING**, trims it or replaces it. Applied metadata survives projects, crash recovery and undo; applying another take never deletes a WAV still referenced by earlier work.
* **VOSPLICE_01 — memes:** full-screen cutaways suspend narration even when music continues. Split the WAV at each insertion, trim in audio seconds and delay each piece in output samples. Game protection uses body time; post-splice music protection uses the shifted pieces. Corner overlays do not insert time.
* **VODEFAULTS_01 — new settings:** both voice-protection policies default to `RememberLastChoice`; the remembered gameplay choice is false and music choice is true. Existing saved policies and remembered values are retained. The checkboxes read **Protect my voice from gameplay sound** and **Protect my voice from background music**.
* **Level & Limiting:** takes keep their recorded level (no normalization, no per-take limiter); the always-on safety limiter on the final mix covers them (PEAKSAFE_01).
* **Sidechain Integration:** Voiceover muxes into the game bus before sidechain trigger generation, so a take also pushes the music down (multiband, §3).
* **Take Management:** Chunks render 40% semi-transparent red overlays on timeline with visual waveform rendering. A take's END on the studio's source-time axis is MAPPED from its captured audio, not added (VOREC_02, `01_TIMELINE_COORDINATE_MATH.md` TL-OUTPUTTIMELINE); the export anchor below is unchanged.
* **Live Timeline Block (VOREC_03):** while a take records, `LiveRecordingCanvas` (film lane, declared AFTER `ThumbLoadingOverlay` — SLIDER_09 declaration order is paint order — so it shows while frames are still generating; `TakeOverlayCanvas` likewise) carries retained visuals created once: the anchor line, the block, the live envelope (≤ one line per pixel column) and a `● REC m:ss.t` label beside the growing edge. Width = `SourceEndForCapturedAudio(StartSec, captured)`, so a dead microphone draws NO block, a freeze hold stops the growth while the clock keeps counting, and a cut is spanned at the join. Segments still draining stay drawn, frozen, until their verdict lands. Tests: `VoiceOverTimelineTests`. Export mixes chunks via `adelay` and `amix`, each delayed to the take's position in OUTPUT time: `granularTimeMapper` maps the take start through speed segments and cuts (without it: take start minus extract start, divided by the base speed), and when the music is mixed after the meme splice the thumbnail intro and any memes inserted before the take are added. A take starting before the body is `atrim`med:
  $$\text{Delay}_{\text{ms}} = \left(\text{granularTimeMapper}(t_{\text{take\_start}}) + t_{\text{intro}} + t_{\text{memes before}}\right) \times 1000$$
* **Live Indicators (VOREC_01 — supersedes "microphone open = pulsing red"):** the status word, the REC lamp, the badge left of the mic button, the mic button's icon/classes and the live timeline block are written by ONE method, `VoiceOverWindow.RefreshRecordingIndicator` (`VoiceOverWindow.RecordingState.cs`), from ONE pure resolution, `ResolveRecordingIndicator`, over the window flags and the capture session's own truth (`VoiceCaptureState`, MICHEALTH_01 health, VOLIVE_01 live take). Priority: closing > Apply (`SAVING…`) > an active take > a latched failure > a draining take (`SAVING TAKE…`) > `TAKE SAVED` (3 s) > readiness. Active-take phases:
  | Phase | Status word | Badge | Lamp shape | Red / live |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | waiting for the video clock | `ARMING` | `ARMING` | ring | no |
  | recorder open queued / opening | `STARTING MIC` | `STARTING` | ring | no |
  | device open, **zero buffers** | `WAITING FOR AUDIO` | `NO AUDIO YET` | ring | no |
  | buffers delivered (silence counts: `SilentData` or `AudibleData`) | `RECORDING` | `REC m:ss.t` (captured audio) | solid dot | **yes** |
  | paused | `PAUSED` | `PAUSED m:ss.t` (frozen) | two bars | no |
  | fault | `MIC ERROR` | `MIC ERROR` | triangle | no |
  * **Opening is not recording.** The open callback used to declare RECORDING the moment `waveInOpen` returned; the field log of 2026-10-05 (zero buffers, 46-byte WAV after success-looking lines) is exactly that lie. RED requires delivered buffers.
  * **The clock is captured audio**, `Σ frames / sample rate` over the run's segments (VOLIVE_01), never the video clock or wall time. Pause banks the segment (`BankLiveSegment`, BEFORE `FinalizeRecordingAsync` retires the meter), so the clock freezes; resume continues the run's total.
  * **Shape + words, not colour alone.** The mic button shows a stop square while a take owns the microphone (`MicStopIcon`) and is named "Stop voiceover recording".
  * **Pulse is a short cue.** `pulse` is added beside `recording` only for the first `RecordingPulseLimit` (10 s) of each live segment (`Button.recording.pulse`, `Path.recording.pulse` in `AvaloniaApp.axaml`); a static red lamp, the glow and the clock remain. Pause, stop, fault and close remove both classes; nothing animates after capture ends.
  * **Readiness words.** `READY` iff the VOMON_02 lamp predicate is true (`_readyLampLit`); otherwise `LOADING PREVIEW`, `PREVIEW OFF`, `NO MIC`, `CONNECTING…`, `CHECKING MIC` (open, no buffers yet), `MIC ERROR` or `MIC UNAVAILABLE`. No code path writes a literal READY.
  * **Failures stay named.** `ShowMicrophoneUnavailable(message, label)` names the cause (`NO MIC`, `MIC ERROR`, `NOT STARTED` when the video never rolled — it used to say `NO MIC` for all). A zero-buffer take latches `NO AUDIO` (never "too short"). A latch clears when a new take starts, or when the microphone is re-opened (`Connecting`) and then delivers buffers.
  * **Closed windows ignore late work.** After `OnClosed` the indicator is `Closed`, classes are removed, the capture handlers are unsubscribed, and a late open verdict, drain verdict, health event or tick changes nothing.
  * Tests: `VoiceOverRecordingUiTests` (headless, production window + production session + UI-dispatcher post target).
* **Live Take Meter (VOLIVE_01, `Services/LiveTakeMeter.cs`):** the take in progress is measured from the recorder's OWN PCM (the SPECTRUM_02 data path; no second device). `VoiceCaptureSession` creates one meter per open attempt and feeds it every buffer from `_recorder` OR `_openingRecorder` (those buffers are in the WAV too), outside `_gate` under the meter's own lock; finalize / release / open failure / dispose retire it, a recorder fault freezes it, and a frozen meter ignores late callbacks. `IVoiceCaptureSession.LiveTake` is a latest-value snapshot the UI samples once per tick — no per-buffer dispatch. Bounded: one peak per 50 ms bucket, at most 2048 buckets; when full, pairs merge (max) and the bucket length doubles. Frames split across callbacks are carried, so chunking never changes the result. PCM 16/24/32 and float32, any channel count (peak across channels).
* **The READY Lamp Reports CAPABILITY, Not Enumeration (VOMON_02):** The green lamp right of Play/Pause is lit only when the studio can actually record:
  $$\text{READY} \iff \text{HasInputDevice} \land \text{PreviewUp} \land \neg\text{Connecting} \land \neg\text{Faulted} \land \neg\text{HasUnreleasedDevice} \land (\text{Recording} \lor (\text{MonitorOpen} \land \text{DeliveredBufferEvidence}))$$
  Enumeration alone is not enough. An endpoint that Windows LISTS but will not OPEN — held exclusively by another app, or blocked by microphone privacy — is the single most common cause of a silent take, and a lamp driven by enumeration shows green while the meter is dead and every take is empty.
  ⚠️ **It must be re-evaluated on the playback tick.** `StartMicMonitor` QUEUES the device open on the serialised audio chain (VOASYNC_02) and returns immediately, so the monitor is always still closed at that instant. A lamp written only at that call site can never become true.
* **Name The Failure, Do Not Swallow It (VOMON_02):** `MicLevelMonitor` only logs a failed open (Warn) and returns quietly, which on screen is indistinguishable from a quiet room. Two one-shot notices are raised the moment each is provable, and both are written to the runtime log:
  * the device could not be opened (raised once the queued open has actually had its turn on the chain);
  * the device is open and has delivered pure digital silence for **6 seconds**.
* **End Of Timeline (VOEND_01):** Parked on MARK END the caret is hidden, and the next PLAY **or RECORD** restarts from MARK START. RECORD especially: `EnforceTrimEndStop` kills a take on the tick after it arms, so without the rewind every take recorded from the end of the clip comes back empty. Full rule: `01_TIMELINE_COORDINATE_MATH.md` §8 (TL-ENDSTOP).
* **Preview Safety Timeout:** Recording aborts after 3.0 seconds if the video preview clock fails to advance, cleaning up empty takes and resetting UI state.
* **Playback Rebuild Key:** Player instances rebuild keyed on target take ID to avoid infinite re-instantiation loops.
* **Preview Player Synchronization:** `VoiceOverPreviewPlayer` only plays takes; it maps start timestamps through `timeMapper` across all windows, adjusting for granular speeds, and follows the video clock (`MpvIpcClient.CurrentTime` / `TimePosChanged`). Take waveforms are drawn by `VoiceOverWindow` with `StreamGeometry`; the studio ticks on a 50ms `DispatcherTimer`. The transport-row meter is the microphone's real frequency spectrum (SPECTRUM_01..04 below) — the old "EQ" that drew every bar as `peak × (0.3 + Random × 0.7)` is gone, and no meter amplitude may come from a random number.
* **Studio Open Anchor:** Studio always opens at `MARK START` (trim-in point).
* **Idle Input Monitor (`MicLevelMonitor.cs`):** Configured for 44.1kHz mono, 50ms buffer. Active during idle; must stop and release WASAPI endpoint before `VoiceRecorder` opens the recording stream.
* **Microphone Frequency Spectrum (SPECTRUM_01..05):** the meter between PAUSE and PLAY shows what the microphone hears, per frequency band. Horizontal position = frequency (low left, high right); bar height = that band's level; colour = level zone. It is a VISUAL indicator only: the studio never plays the microphone back (no monitoring loopback — feedback risk).
  * **`SPECTRUM_01` — analyzer (`Core/Media/MicrophoneSpectrumAnalyzer.cs`).** Input is the real PCM of each capture buffer with its real `PcmCaptureFormat` (production: 44.1 kHz / mono / PCM16). Supported: PCM 16/24/32-bit and IEEE float 32-bit, 1–8 channels (averaged to mono), 8–384 kHz; anything else throws `NotSupportedException` — float or stereo bytes are never reinterpreted as mono PCM16. Samples (and a partial trailing frame) are carried across callbacks in a fixed ring, so spectra are identical however the driver chunks the stream.
    * FFT 4096 samples, periodic Hann, hop 1024: window 92.9 ms, a new spectrum every 23.2 ms of audio, **resolution one bin = SampleRate / 4096 = 10.77 Hz at 44.1 kHz**. No bar claims finer resolution than that.
    * 40 bands, log-spaced from 60 Hz to min(16 kHz, Nyquist − 2 bins). A band narrower than one bin is widened to exactly one bin and the remaining range re-spaced logarithmically, so every band owns ≥ 1 distinct bin and no bin belongs to two bands (at 44.1 kHz bands 0–3 are single bins: 60–71, 71–82, 82–94, 94–108 Hz). Edges are recomputed on every sample-rate change.
    * Band level = Σ|X_k|² over the band's bins ÷ (N·Σw²/4), in dB: a full-scale sine whose energy lies in one band reads 0 dBFS wherever it sits between bins; halving amplitude reads −6.02 dB. Silence floors at −120 dBFS (no log(0)/NaN/∞). A tone near a band edge legitimately splits across the two neighbouring bands (Hann main lobe ±2 bins).
    * Display scale: level = clamp((dBFS + 60) / 60, 0, 1). Values above 0 dBFS (harmonics of clipped input) clamp at 1.
    * Ballistics (`SpectrumMeterBallistics`, UI side, caller-supplied clock): attack τ = 20 ms, release τ = 220 ms; a snapshot that has not been REPLACED for 300 ms counts as absent and the bars release to zero; below 0.002 a bar is empty, and a settled meter requests no repaint.
  * **`SPECTRUM_02` — one data path (`VoiceCaptureSession`).** `IMicMonitorDevice`/`IVoiceRecorderDevice` expose `PcmAvailable` (the adapters re-raise with themselves as sender). ONE analyzer serves idle monitoring and recording. A buffer is admitted only from the current monitor while `Monitoring`/`Connecting`, or the live `_recorder` while `Recording`; every source change (monitor open/re-open/device switch, stop, recorder take-over, finalize, release, fault, dispose) bumps `_spectrumEpoch` and clears `LatestSpectrum`, and a result is published only if its admission epoch is still current — a stale device can never paint the display. The FFT runs on the capture thread under `_spectrumGate` (never `_gate`, never the dispatcher); publication is a single immutable-snapshot reference (`LatestSpectrum`) the UI samples on its tick — no per-buffer dispatch, no backlog.
  * **`SPECTRUM_05` — a retired source never publishes, not even its first spectrum (`VoiceCaptureSession`).** Audit regression (2026-10-09): the first buffer of a fresh monitor passed admission, the monitor then stopped or faulted while that FFT was in flight, and retirement only bumped the epoch "if a snapshot was held" — none was yet, so the in-flight result published into an `Idle`/`Faulted` session. Snapshot presence is not evidence that nothing is in flight. Rules: (1) every transition into `Idle`/`Faulted`/`Disposed` retires the epoch UNCONDITIONALLY (`UpdateHealthLocked`), alongside every explicit source change; (2) publication re-checks, under `_gate`, BOTH the admission epoch AND `IsSpectrumSourceEligibleLocked(source)` — the same predicate admission uses (current monitor in `Monitoring`/`Connecting`, or current recorder in `Recording`); (3) a buffer whose epoch is already stale when it reaches the analyzer is dropped before it can reset the analyzer its successor is filling. `_gate` and `_spectrumGate` are never held together; the FFT runs under `_spectrumGate` only; `SpectrumAvailable` subscribers run with neither lock. Test seam: `SpectrumAdmittedForTesting` (after admission, before analysis). Tests: `MicrophoneSpectrumAuditTests` (monitor stop/fault, no prior snapshot) and `VoiceCaptureSessionTests.Spectrum_FirstRecorderBufferInFlight_*`, `Spectrum_DisposeWithFirstBufferInFlight_*`, `Spectrum_RetiredBufferInFlight_CannotOverwriteOrResetTheFreshSource`, `Spectrum_FreshMonitorAfterFault_StillPublishes`.
  * **`SPECTRUM_03` — the control (`Controls/MicrophoneSpectrumControl.cs`).** Draws directly in `Render` (no child controls, no per-tick layout): one narrow bottom-aligned bar per band, equal widths, constant 1–2 px gaps; lit segments green below −18 dBFS (level 0.7), amber to −6 dBFS (0.9), red above; unlit space is the dark meter face. Colours come only from `AppMeterBackgroundBrush`, `AppMeterUnlitBrush`, `AppMeterNormalBrush`, `AppMeterWarningBrush`, `AppMeterPeakBrush`, `AppMeterLabelBrush` (both theme dictionaries), resolved in the control's own `ActualThemeVariant`. Labels `LOW · 60 Hz`, `1 kHz` (over the band that contains 1 kHz, only when it fits) and `HIGH · 16 kHz` come from the actual band edges.
  * **`SPECTRUM_04` — the studio (`VoiceOverWindow`).** The track is 68 px tall (was 34) with `MinWidth=140`, so the bars stay legible at the 980×640 floor. `Timer_Tick` calls `UpdateSpectrumMeter` once per tick, which passes `LatestSpectrum` — or null while closing, committed or `Faulted`. A meme cutaway changes the picture, not the microphone: the cutaway branch of `Timer_Tick` still runs `CheckHealth`, `UpdateReadyLamp` and `UpdateSpectrumMeter` before returning. Readiness text and the lamp (MICHEALTH_01) are unchanged: a healthy silent microphone shows READY with empty bars; a failed one shows the error with bars decaying to empty.
  * Tests: `MicrophoneSpectrumAnalyzerTests` (Core: silence, 125 Hz/1 kHz/8 kHz placement, two tones, −6.02 dB, clipping, chunk/odd-byte invariance, reset, sample-rate change, unsupported formats, bounded state, ballistics), `MicrophoneSpectrumControlTests` (headless geometry, zones, low/high separation, resize, theme resolution, decay, window wiring) and the `Spectrum_*` cases in `VoiceCaptureSessionTests`. Native verification on a real microphone remains manual (speak/hum, silence decay, device switch, minimum size, high DPI, both themes).
* **Voice Protection System (VOPROT_01 / VOGATE_01):** Independent toggles protect the voice from the game and from the music. Across every take (0.3 s ramps either side) the protected bus is ducked 85% (`volume='1.0-0.85*pulse'`) and carved -3 dB at 2.5 kHz — the carve is GATED by the same pulse (`AudioFilterChain.GatedVoiceProtection`: a carved copy faded in by the pulse + the dry copy faded out by its complement), so outside the takes the bus is untouched (it used to be a static EQ over the whole video). Priority VOPRIO_01: voice > gameplay > music. Settings persist choices (`ON`, `OFF`, `REMEMBER LAST CHOICE`).
* **Deleted Footage Seek-Skip (CUTS_02):** Cut spans are drawn on the studio's own timeline and SKIPPED in a single seek during preview playback. The studio's playback tick performs that skip BEFORE anything else it does — a tick that has wandered into deleted footage is reasoning about a frame that is not in the finished video.
* **Cross-Cut Take Warning (CUTS_02):** A take whose recorded span crosses one or more cut boundaries MUST raise `"Skipped a deleted section"`. Voice-over is anchored to the video clock; speech recorded across frames that do not exist in the export desyncs severely at render time. The take is kept — the user is warned, not silently corrected.
* **Companion Audio Pauses With A Meme Cutaway (MEME_07):** While `MemePreviewDirector.IsActive`, every voice-over take and the music bed pause, and resume when gameplay returns. Cutaways are additionally SUSPENDED outright during arming and recording: cutting away mid-take would anchor speech to frames the take never heard. Authoritative rule: `03_FFMPEG_EXPORT_PIPELINE.md` §5 (FFM-MEMEPREVIEW).
* **Thread-Bound Safety Contract:** All WASAPI operations run on an isolated serialized background worker thread off the UI dispatcher.
* **One Owner For The Microphone (VOCAPTURE_01):** `VoiceCaptureSession` (App/Services) owns the `VoiceRecorder`, the `MicLevelMonitor`, the VOASYNC_02 device chain, the pending take drains, cancellation, disposal and the fault state. `VoiceOverWindow` keeps controls, lamps, meters, dialogs and timeline drawing, and talks to the session only through `IVoiceCaptureSession`; the session references no `Window`, `Control`, `Canvas` or `Dispatcher` (results come back through an injected post delegate).
  * States: `Idle`, `Monitoring`, `StartingRecording`, `Recording`, `StoppingRecording`, `Faulted`, `Disposed`.
  * ⚠️ **VOASYNC_02 is preserved, not replaced:** every blocking device call (monitor open/close, recorder open/drain/dispose) is appended to ONE ordered `ContinueWith` chain on the thread pool. The recorder-open job stops the monitor FIRST inside the same job, so the monitor and the recorder can never hold the device at the same time. A second open while one is in flight or live is refused. Stop/release/dispose during an open cancels it; the opened device is closed and its WAV deleted on the chain.
  * A finalized take's verdict callback runs (posted to the UI thread) BEFORE `WhenFinalizationsSettled` completes, so Apply and the close prompt always see the take. A failed open leaves the session `Faulted` with `LastFault` set and no device held; `StartMonitorAsync` recovers it. Dispose drains the recorder exactly once and unsubscribes every device handler.
  * **`VOCAPTURE_02` — publish, THEN complete.** `StartRecordingAsync` and `FinalizeRecordingAsync` complete their returned Task only AFTER `onSettled` has run on the post target, inside the same posted action (`PostThenComplete`). Previously the Task completed first and the callback was posted second, so an awaiting caller could resume before the result was published. The callback runs at most once; completion still happens if the callback throws or the post target refuses the work (callback skipped, no hang).
  * **`VOAPPLY_03` — Unconditional Settlement & Safe Device Ownership:** Unhandled exceptions during monitor stop abort recorder creation, latch session fault, and ensure no concurrent device handle is requested. Unhandled exceptions during counter reads or recorder disposal are trapped or finalized in `finally` blocks so `WhenFinalizationsSettled` settles deterministically. Production adapters (`NAudioVoiceCaptureDevices`) re-raise inner driver events with the adapter instance as `sender` so reference identity checks and buffer counters never drop events. Device release failures latch `_hasUnreleasedDevice = true`, preventing subsequent capture or monitor acquisition until safe.
  * **`VOAPPLY_01` — Single-Flight Apply, Persistence Rollback & Failed Take Recovery:** Apply is single-flight (`_isApplying`). Capture finalization is awaited with a bounded timeout; if capture drain times out, the commit is aborted, no takes are deleted, and recordings remain recoverable on subsequent apply. Committed apply latches `_isCommitted = true` and `_isSafeToClose = true`, permanently locking window mutations and allowing modal resolution while `OnWindowClosing` guards in-flight persistence against user closure. Unresolved failed takes or unreleased capture devices block Apply and are surfaced through dedicated UI affordances (`DiscardFailedTakesButton`, `RecoverFailedTakesButton`):
    * **`VORECOVERY_01` — the failed-take recovery transaction (`Services/VoiceOverRecoveryManager.cs`).** Three separate predicates, never one: `BlocksApply` (`Pending`, `Validating`), `MustRemainDurable` (everything except `Committed`/`Discarded`) and `IsTerminal` (its record may leave storage). `DiscardedByUser` is a legacy alias, not a persistence decision.

      | State | Blocks Apply | Durable | Leaves storage when |
      | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
      | `Pending` (capture, validation, storage or publication error) | yes | yes | explicit proven discard, or a later verified commit |
      | `Validating` | yes | yes | same; a failed validation returns to `Pending` |
      | `RecoveredInSession` | no | yes | successful Apply commit of THIS take, or explicit discard |
      | `Committed` / `Discarded` | no | no | immediately, per take id |

      * **Errors are typed (`RecoveryErrorKind`).** A rejected UI publication (window applying/committed/closing, or a throwing callback) reverts the take to `Pending` with `Publication` — never a corrupt-audio diagnosis. Validation reads the RIFF header, requires a non-zero sample count and duration and at least one readable sample block (the old `> 44 bytes` size check is only the first gate).
      * **Storage and identity.** One index per (canonical video, recording session): `voiceover_recovery_{videoKey}_{sessionId}.json`, schema version 2, with a serialized stable take `id`, `wavPath`, finite `startSec`/`endSec`, `trimLeftSec`/`trimRightSec`, `isMuted`, `reason`, `errorKind`, `state`. An empty/invalid video identity maps to the dedicated `unbound` key and only matches other unbound indexes — never a shared `default`. Legacy `voiceover_recovery_{videoKey}.json` / global `voiceover_recovery_manifest.json` entries are adopted only on exact canonical-video match; an entry without an id gets a deterministic `legacy-{hash(wavPath)}` id so adoption and rewrite stay idempotent.
      * **Writes cannot lose another owner's records.** Writes are process-serialized; each rewrites a file by MERGING — entries whose id this manager does not hold are preserved verbatim — then writes a unique `{path}.{guid}.tmp` with `FileOptions.WriteThrough` + `Flush(true)` and atomically replaces. A newer snapshot is never overwritten by an older one (per-file generation). An existing index that cannot be parsed is left untouched and the write fails truthfully. A failed write keeps the previous valid file and all in-memory takes.
      * **Discovery is evidence-preserving.** Unreadable indexes are reported (`LoadErrors`, shown to the user) and never become rewritable, so they are never deleted. A record whose WAV is gone is still adopted as a `Pending`/`Storage` take ("audio missing") the user must explicitly discard. Re-running discovery never duplicates a take (id or path).
      * **Nothing on the dispatcher.** Register, recover, discard, commit and reopen discovery snapshot state on the UI thread, run ALL file-system work through `IVoiceOverRecoveryStore` on the thread pool, and publish results back on the UI thread. Every operation returns a truthful `RecoveryStoreResult` / `RecoveryBatchResult.StorageError`; a failed index write is surfaced in the hint and a notice with the audio still on disk.
      * **One owner at a time (`IsOperationInFlight`).** While recover/discard/discovery owns the take list, Apply, the other recovery buttons and a second click are refused, and a window close is DEFERRED (`_closeRequestedDuringRecovery`) and re-issued when the operation ends — a caller that stops waiting never ends ownership. Apply in turn refuses to start while an operation is in flight; reopen discovery therefore also blocks Apply until every durable take of this video is on screen.
      * **Commit boundary.** Apply commits when every take has been persisted to its new file in `ProgramData\voiceovers`; only the committed take ids then leave the index (`CommitTakesAsync`), other sessions' records and still-pending takes are untouched. A failed index cleanup after that point does not undo the save (the copies are delivered in `Result`); a stale record reopens as an explicit "audio missing" entry, never as silent loss. A failed Apply commits nothing: recovered takes stay `RecoveredInSession` and durable, and reopen shows them again with the same id and timing. Cancel/close keeps durable takes on disk (`DeleteUnappliedVoiceOverFiles` skips them).
      * **`VORECOVERY_02` — terminal resolution dominates stale snapshots.** Two windows/managers can adopt the same take; a manager that loaded it earlier keeps a stale `Pending` view. A commit/discard is therefore written FIRST to `voiceover_tombstones_{videoKey}.json` (id → `Committed`/`Discarded`, UTC; 30-day retention), and only then is the record removed from its manifest. Every write drops durable records and preserved entries whose id is tombstoned and returns them in `RecoveryStoreResult.ResolvedElsewhere`; the stale manager adopts the terminal state (never re-blocks Apply). Discovery never adopts a tombstoned id, even from a manifest whose cleanup failed or that was deleted and rewritten. If the tombstone cannot be read or written, NOTHING is removed and the save fails truthfully. ## 5. Multi-Clip Concat Synchronization  {#AUD-CONCAT}
* **A/V Lock:** Input clips join as unified audio/video segments, re-zeroing together at boundaries.
* **Silence For Silent Clips:** A clip with no audio stream gets synthesized silence (`anullsrc`) for exactly its length, so every clip contributes an audio segment to the concat.
* **Exact Clip Length (CLIPFRAMES_01):** When the edit list describes the queue, every plain clip's video is bounded to the composite's frame count (`tpad=stop_mode=clone:stop=1,trim=end_frame={n}` — a cloned frame pads a short one) and its audio is padded and cut to the same length (`apad,atrim=end={n/60}`), so the file, the preview and the music agree to the frame and no desync accumulates across boundaries:
  $$n = \operatorname{round}(\text{OutputLength}_{\text{clip}} \times 60), \quad t_{\text{audio}} = n / 60$$
* **Merger Music On The Output Clock (MUSICMAP_01):** Music start/end placed on the merged clock (Music Wizard, Merger preview) is mapped through `CompositeTimeline.MergedSecToBodyOutputSec`, so speed ramps, freezes, memes and cuts before that moment move it exactly as they move the video. The music itself is never stretched. Without a matching edit list it falls back to merged seconds ÷ base speed.
* **Offset Rebase:** Clips with asynchronous audio/video start offsets are re-zeroed prior to concatenation.

## 7. Background Music Bed Fades & Meme Continuation  {#AUD-MUSICFADE}
* **Music Fades Are Independent Of Video Fades:** The master `Enable Fades` toggle governs the VIDEO only. `AudioFilterChain` gives the music bed a linear fade-in at its very start and a linear fade-out at its very end (`EdgeFadeSec` = 1.5s), each capped at half the track. Either edge fade can be off (ISSUE_04): with the music-start note marker dragged right of MARK START the music enters at full level, and with the music-end marker dragged right of MARK END it is cut dead at MARK END.
* **Song-To-Song Crossfade:** Between songs the outgoing song fades out over its last 7s (`CrossfadeOutSec`) and the incoming song starts 3s (`CrossfadeInSec`) before that end, fading in across the overlap; both are capped at half a track.
* **XFADEPLAN_01 — the crossfade is in the PLAN, not only in the graph.** The two numbers and the overlap formula live in `MusicCrossfade` (`Overlap(cur, prev) = max(0, min(3, min(cur, prev)/2))`), read by `AudioFilterChain`, `MusicBedPlan`, the wizard's song lane and both live previews. The chain reads `Duration + overlap` seconds from each song, so the plan gives every song after the first a `LeadInSec` taken OUT of its window:
  $$\text{lead}_i = \min\left(3,\ \frac{D_{i-1}}{2},\ \frac{W_i}{3}\right), \qquad D_i = W_i - \text{lead}_i$$
  which the chain's own formula reproduces from the durations it is handed (when the bed ends inside a song, $D_i$ is cut and the lead recomputed from it). Before this, a song's whole remaining length was handed over as `Duration`; the chain then asked for 3 s more than the file had, every song ran dry 3 s before its slot ended and the next rose out of silence — a hole between every pair of songs and 3 silent seconds at the end. Guarded by `MusicPlaylistTests.TheExportNeverReadsPastTheEndOfASongsWindow`.
* **Continuation Across A Meme:** With `KeepMusicDuringMeme` on and memes present, the music is mixed AFTER the meme splice: each track's duration STRETCHES by the total meme length (D'_music = D_music + D_meme) and its start shifts by the meme time inserted before it. A master audio fade (at most 1s, and at most half the last meme) is synthesized over the FINAL FRAMES only when `Enable Fades` is on and the video ends on a meme of at least 0.5s.
* **The question is always asked (MEMEMUSIC_02):** "keep the music under the meme?" is asked once music and any FULL SCREEN meme (editor or main-screen) are both present and it was never answered, and again on every Music Wizard finish (how it is changed).
* **Corner meme sound ducks the game (MEMEDUCK_01):** while a corner meme's own sound plays, the game audio is lowered to 0.5 (−6 dB) by one `volume=…:eval=frame` before the `amix` (`CornerMemeOverlayGraph.DuckGain`); music and voice-over are untouched.
* **Speed Independence:** Music fade lengths are human-time constants and are NOT multiplied by the segment speed factor. Only VIDEO fade padding is speed-scaled (PadStartHumanSec x S — see `03_FFMPEG_EXPORT_PIPELINE.md` §6, FFM-FADES).

## 8. Preview Audio Follows The Video In Output Time  {#AUD-PREVIEWSYNC}
* **MUSICPAD_01 — the fade-in pad is part of the export body, so the music moves with it.** The Main App places the music in output seconds from MARK START (the preview's clock), but with fades on `ProcessWorker` extracts up to 1 s BEFORE MARK START (`padStartHumanSec`) and mixes the music into that padded body. Every track was therefore one pad EARLY in the file (measured 1.000 s on a 3–9 s trim at 1.1x: onset 1.923 s vs 2.918 s). `MusicPadAlignment.Align` (called in ProcessWorker right after the track list is built) adds the lead pad to every track's delay; a bed that starts ON MARK START with the lead fade pre-rolls into the fade-in from earlier in the song (song stays frame-aligned); a bed that reaches MARK END with the tail fade runs on through the fade-out pad. Voice-over takes were already measured from the padded start. Harness after the fix: onset 2.923 s vs 2.918 s predicted.
* **MUSICWIN_01 — every song plays its chosen part.** The plan takes one `MusicBedTrack(path, knownSec, inSec, outSec)` per song (`MusicWizardResult.ToBedTracks()`, the ONE reader: per-song values are indexed by the song's ORIGINAL position before missing files are dropped). Results without windows (older projects, sessions, recovery files) read as the legacy layout: first song from `OffsetSeconds`, others from 0, all to the end. The Main App export and preview, and the merger export and preview, all build their bed from it; the merger export hands the worker the finished bed (`MergerWorker.MusicTracksArePlanned`) instead of letting it re-probe and re-loop bare file names. A preview segment's file position at its boundary includes its lead (`Locate` = `FileOffsetSec + LeadInSec + t`); the Add Music preview reproduces the outgoing fade with `OutgoingGainAt` on its single player.
* **MUSICSYNC_01:** the music bed plan (`MusicBedPlan.Build`) is shared by the export and the live preview. The preview locates "which file, which second" from `TimelineViewModel.PreviewSourceToOutputSeconds`, the same OutputTimeline mapping as the export, non-mutating and cached. Before, the preview used raw source seconds and played one file, so it disagreed with the export by the base speed, every cut and freeze, and every track after the first.
* **MUSICSYNC_02:** ⚠ **THE MUSIC ALWAYS PLAYS AT ITS OWN NORMAL SPEED (1.0x). Only the video speed changes.** The music player's `speed` is pinned to 1.0 on every start and never adjusted. Drift is corrected by SEEKING only (`PreviewAudioSync`): 0.12 s tolerance, confirmed on 2 consecutive ticks. Voice-over takes use `PreviewAudioSync.CreateVoicePlayer` (120 ms buffering) and compensate the reader lead.

## 9. Add Music: Every Song, Every Part, One Layout  {#AUD-MUSICWIN}
* **The model (PLAYLIST_01):** an ordered list of `MusicPlaylistEntry(path, fullSec, inSec, outSec, userTrimmed)`. The first entry's `inSec` is what every older path calls "the song start" (`_songStartSeconds` is a property over it). Shortest window: `MusicPlaylistMath.MinWindowSec` = 2 s.

* **Coverage:** `MusicCoverage` — silent tail below 0.5 s counts as covered; `Percent` rounds DOWN so a gap never reads 100%; "looping" only when the bed actually repeated a song.
* **Editing (SONGLANE_01 / SONGCARD_01):** the right edge of a song's block on the music lane is that song's `outSec`; on the song card, the bright box is `[inSec, outSec]` — drag inside = slide (same length), drag an edge = trim, click outside = the box starts there; on release "Snap to beat" moves `inSec` onto the nearest peak within 1 s. * **Persistence:** `MusicWizardResult.MusicStartsSeconds` / `MusicEndsSeconds` (0 = to the end) travel through the Main App recovery file (`musicStartsSeconds` / `musicEndsSeconds`), the merger edit list (`EdlMusic.StartsSec` / `EndsSec`, filled empty for older files — EDLNULL_01) and `MergerMusicState`. * **AI Music Auto-Fit — honest choice, local start (`AIMUSIC_HONEST_01`, `AIMUSIC_START_01`, 2026-10-16):** the AI cannot hear the songs, so it is asked ONLY to choose: a NUMBERED list (title — artist (length), up to `MaxSongsInPrompt` = 150, the prompt says when more exist) and the answer is `songNumber` (+ mood, reason). The old free-text name match let an empty or short title match the wrong song and silently fell back to song #1 while still showing the AI's reason; now an unknown answer changes nothing and says so (`AiMusicAutoFitException`). The START is measured locally (`AiMusicAutoFitService.PickStartSec`): the song's loudness (`MusicEnergyAnalysis`, 50 ms buckets) in 2 s windows; the first window reaching 60 % of the loudest is where it "gets going"; playback starts 0.5 s earlier, clamped to `songLength − videoLength` so the song never runs out before the video (0 when the song is shorter — the sentence then suggests a second song or loop). The old AI-guessed `inSec` could leave the end silent (150 s into a 180 s song under a 60 s video). Progress and limits: `04` UI-MUSICWIZARD (`AIMUSIC_PROGRESS_01`).
* **AI Music Auto-Fit frames and length (`AIMUSIC_SCENES_01`, `AIMUSIC_UNDO_01`):** the AI sees frames from the WHOLE finished video — Main App: 12 / 35 / 60 / 85 % of the output, each mapped back to its source second; Merger: 4–8 frames spread over EVERY clip's kept window by length (it used to read clip 1 only, and times past clip 1's end failed). The chosen song part is sized to the FINISHED length (`GetPhase3VideoDurationSeconds`: speed changes, freezes, cuts), not the source length. Applying the pick is ONE undo step (`EndWizardGesture`); it used to replace the history baseline, so Ctrl+Z could not bring the previous songs back.
